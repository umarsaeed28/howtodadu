# Access and Driveways

> Team guide for Seattle DADU screening. Not copied from the Seattle Municipal Code.

Also called: backyard cottage, DADU, detached accessory dwelling unit, detached ADU.

## Alley access
- A back alley is ideal for a 40 ft wide lot. People and cars reach the rear unit from the alley, so no lot width is spent on a driveway.

## No alley
- Without an alley, vehicle access has to come from the front street along the side of the existing house.
- On a 40 ft wide lot a side driveway is very tight. The existing house must leave enough clearance on one side.
- Lots 45 ft wide or more are far better suited to a side driveway.

## No vehicle access: excluded (team rule)
The team does not pursue a lot where no car can reach the back: no alley, not a corner lot, and no room beside the house for a 10 ft driveway. Such a lot is excluded.

The app measures the side room from the city's 2023 building outlines. They are traced from rooftops, so they include the eaves, and the ground is usually a foot or two wider. A roofline gap under 8 ft is treated as blocked. A gap of 8 to 10 ft may still fit a driveway at ground level, so confirm it on site. When the app cannot measure it, confirm on site that a driveway fits beside the house.

## Vehicle access and marketability
- Units with their own vehicle or garage access sell and rent for noticeably more.
- A rear unit reached only on foot is legal, but it tends to sell poorly in the Seattle market. Flag it as a resale risk.
