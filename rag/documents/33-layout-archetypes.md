# Layout Archetypes

> Team guide for Seattle DADU screening. Not copied from the Seattle Municipal Code.

Also called: backyard cottage, DADU, detached accessory dwelling unit, detached ADU.

## Single rear DADU
The main house sits at the front and the DADU at the back, with a fence between them. This is the standard layout for a 40 ft by 100 ft lot.

## Side by side
- Needs a lot at least 45 ft wide, ideally 50 ft or wider.
- The house and the DADU both face the street or alley, each with its own street-facing door and mailbox.
- Buyers like it. Guests find the units easily, and it resells better than an interior or rear unit.

## Staggered front to back
- Best for deep lots of 120 ft or more that are wide enough to offset the buildings.
- It keeps a yard for each unit, lets sunlight in, and keeps vehicle access to the interior unit.

## Stacked in a straight line (high risk)
- Units placed one directly behind another make dark, cramped interior units. They block sunlight, sightlines and neighbors' access.
- Interior units with no light and no yard are hard to sell. Treat this layout as a warning, not an option.
