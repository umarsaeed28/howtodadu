# Lot Assessment Workflow

> How to assess a lot, in order. Use only the facts you are given and the passages you retrieve.

## Steps
1. Area: is the lot at least 3,200 sq ft?
2. Dimensions: how wide is it (40 ft, or 45 to 55 ft or more) and how deep? Width decides which layouts fit.
3. Access: is there an alley, or does a car have to come in from the front street?
4. Layout: pick the best archetype: single rear, side by side, or staggered. Never recommend a straight stack.
5. Livability and resale: check sunlight, yard space, vehicle access and marketability.

## Report sections
A lot assessment covers four things: a quick feasibility summary (area pass or fail, width and depth, alley yes, no or unknown), layout and site planning (recommended layout, driveway or alley strategy, height), livability and resale risk (sunlight and sightlines, vehicle and parking access, yard space, marketability high, moderate or low), and next steps before buying.

## Next steps before buying
1. Verify exact side setbacks and the position of the primary house for driveway clearance.
2. Confirm how sewer, water and electric connect.
3. Talk to an architect or builder who specializes in Seattle DADUs.

## Rule for missing numbers
If a numerical limit is not in the knowledge base, say so plainly. Do not make up setbacks, lot coverage or other code figures.
