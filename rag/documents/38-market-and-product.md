# Market Dynamics and Product Selection

> Team notes from a Seattle builder's talk. Market observations, not code. Figures are not verified.

Also called: backyard cottage, DADU, ADU, infill housing, step-up home.

## Products to avoid (oversupplied)
- Skinny townhomes: narrow, multi-story units 12 to 16 ft wide. There are too many of them, and buyers want functional layouts.
- Small two-bedroom units of about 1,000 sq ft. They have been built at about two for every single-family home. Supply is high and demand is low, because buyers want room to work from home.

## The undersupplied sweet spot: the step-up home
- The gap is upper-middle-class infill housing between $2M+ single-family scrape-and-rebuilds and $900K small ADUs.
- Target size: 1,300 to 1,600 sq ft.
- Target price: $950,000 to $1,200,000.

## What this means for a DADU
A DADU near the old 1,000 sq ft cap competes with the oversupplied small two-bedroom units. A larger unit with a third bedroom or an office fits the step-up gap better, where the lot allows it.
